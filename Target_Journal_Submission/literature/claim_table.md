## Key facts from the two dataset papers (read in full text)

- **CARE score (Gück, Roelofs, Faulstich, *Data* 2024, 9, 138, doi:10.3390/data9120138).** CARE is a weighted mean of four parts. Coverage is a pointwise F_beta with beta = 1/2 on the anomaly datasets. Accuracy is tn/(fp+tn) on the normal datasets, with weight 2. Reliability is an event-based F_beta with beta = 1/2: a dataset counts as "alarm" if its maximum criticality reaches 72 (about 12 h of consecutive anomalies). Earliness is a weighted score. **Reliability is therefore not a false-alarm rate per year or per asset.** It is an event-wise F0.5 on a roughly 50/50 set of selected windows (44 anomaly + 51 normal in the paper; the Zenodo v6 text says 45/50). The paper presents the balanced design as a strength.
- **PreDist (Roelofs et al., *Energy* 2026, doi:10.1016/j.energy.2026.141178, arXiv 2511.14791).** Reliability is an eventwise F0.5 over 73 faults and 65 normal events. The criticality threshold is chosen by 5-fold stratified CV *over the evaluated events*. The authors write: "the reliability score is overestimated, since we use a balanced dataset ... In reality, the fraction of faults is much lower ... precision is likely lower under realistic class imbalance. Because there is no canonical normal-to-fault ratio, we retain a 50/50 split." **The qualitative form of C1 is therefore stated in the dataset paper itself.** M1 and M2 are *control-unit* manufacturers, so C5 should say "control-unit manufacturer". The CARE farms are anonymised, and that the CARE farms come from different manufacturers is UNVERIFIED.

## Three most threatening items
1. **PreDist paper** 10.1016/j.energy.2026.141178. It states the C1 mechanism qualitatively, and it tunes the threshold by CV on the evaluated events, which matters for how C3 is framed.
2. **Song 2026, "Detection without discrimination" (reproducibility package 10.5281/zenodo.23902231; manuscript submitted to RESS, unreviewed).** It covers alarm budgets on CARE, a random-alarm reference and cross-farm transfer, so it overlaps C2, C3 and C5.
3. **Haneef et al. 2026** 10.3390/info17090930. It shows same-asset calibration gives optimistic thresholds and excessive false alarms, using a cross-asset protocol on CARE (C3, C5). Close runners-up: Liu et al. WES-D 10.5194/wes-2026-145 and Salem & Ferrugem 10.55972/spectrum.v27i1.439.
